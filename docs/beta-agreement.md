# Selodía Beta Agreement

**Version 1.1 · [date]**

This agreement is between Selodía Ltd ("we") and you, as a member of the Selodía beta. While you're in the beta, it applies instead of our Terms of Service. Our Privacy Policy still applies in full.

## 1. Who can join

You need to be 18 or over and live in the UK.

## 2. What you get

Free access to Selodía for as long as you're in the beta. No payment, no card, and nothing that quietly turns into a subscription.

## 3. What "beta" means

Selodía is unfinished. Things will break, sometimes things that worked yesterday. Entries can be lost: we back everything up and we're careful, but it has happened, and if it happens to you we'll tell you. If something you log really matters, keep a copy somewhere else too. Features will change or disappear, sometimes without warning.

## 4. Selodía uses AI, and it can be wrong

Selodía's replies are written by AI, based on what you've logged. They can be wrong. If something doesn't match your record or your experience, please tell us: that's one of the most useful things you can do.

## 5. Selodía is not a medical service

Selodía isn't a doctor, dietitian or medical device, and nothing it says is medical advice. It doesn't replace professional care. If you're worried about your health, talk to your GP. In an emergency, call 999, or 111 if it's urgent but not an emergency.

## 6. Your feedback

Nothing is compulsory. There's a Beta feedback section in the app where you can tell us anything, whenever you like: add a screenshot, write a note, or just tap how something made you feel. Nothing needs filling in before you send. Now and then we may ask a short optional question in the app, which you're free to ignore. If you share an idea, we're free to use it in Selodía without owing you anything, though we'll always be grateful.

## 7. Your data

It's yours. You can export it or delete your account at any time, in the app or at selodia.app/delete-account. We handle it as the Privacy Policy describes, including health information, which we treat with extra care. Being in the beta gives us no extra rights over your data. We don't sell it. When we look at anything you've written, it's only to understand a specific problem, and it never leaves Selodía Ltd.

## 8. How long the beta lasts

There's no fixed end date. You can leave whenever you like, with no notice. If we change or end the beta, we'll give you at least 30 days' notice. Testers whose feedback has helped shape Selodía may be offered continued free access after the beta; we'd like to do that for as long as we can.

## 9. When the beta ends

Your record doesn't disappear. If you don't take up a subscription (or continued free access, if offered), your account becomes read-only: you can still see and export everything, but can't add anything new. You can delete your account instead, at any time.

## 10. No secrecy

There's no confidentiality agreement. Tell anyone you like about Selodía, show it to anyone, and say what you honestly think, in public if you want.

## 11. Ending your access

You can leave at any time by deleting your account. We may end someone's beta access, or the whole beta, with reasonable notice, except where we have to act immediately for a legal or safety reason. Your data is handled as in clause 9.

## 12. Liability

Selodía is provided as it is. As far as the law allows, we're not liable for loss or damage from using it, including lost data or decisions made on what it showed you.

## 13. Changes to this agreement

If this agreement changes, we'll tell you in the app and ask you to accept the new version. Carrying on using Selodía doesn't count as accepting; pressing the button does.

## 14. Law

England and Wales.

## Notes, not part of the agreement

**Three things are still placeholders** and the agreement cannot be shown to anybody until they are filled: the **version date**, the **registered address** and the **company number**. All three follow incorporation.

**Clause 12 is the one to watch.** The earlier draft carried the standard carve-out — that nothing limits liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, for fraud, or for anything else that cannot lawfully be limited. Ruth shortened it deliberately, and the note above flags it for the solicitor. Worth knowing that the carve-out is generally implied by law in England and Wales whether or not it is written, so the shortening changes how the clause reads more than what it does — but that is exactly the kind of sentence a solicitor should confirm rather than me.

**Clause 9's read-only state** is the one a template would have left out, and the one a tester will actually ask about. It is also the clause that depends on the price existing, because "if you don't take up a subscription" has to refer to something real.

**Acceptance is versioned.** The app records which version was accepted and when, through the same mechanism as the health-data consent. Clause 13 says pressing the button is what counts and carrying on using the app is not, so the record has to be able to prove which one happened.
