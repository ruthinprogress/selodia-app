# Ruth's Decision Patterns
*Started 13 August 2026. This document exists to capture recurring patterns in how Ruth actually makes decisions — not rules imposed on her, but observations of judgment already demonstrated across this project. Unlike WORKFLOW.md (process) or SELODIA_SPEC.md's Core Values (product principles), this is about the person making the decisions, and is meant to eventually outgrow this one project — likely extracted into its own standalone reference once there's enough real material to draw from, same treatment as the Development Workflow Principles.*

*Built incrementally, as real decisions happen — not written all at once. Each entry should trace back to an actual moment, not a general impression.*

## Observed patterns so far

**Precision over reassurance.** When a vague or comforting phrase is offered, Ruth consistently pushes for something clearer, even when the vague version is easier to accept in the moment. ("That's saved for you too, by the way" → rejected as unclear, even though it was gentler-sounding; "doesn't land on your legs" → corrected to the accurate physiological explanation rather than kept as a comforting myth.)

**Evidence over assumption, including correcting her own instinct.** Repeatedly asked for real research before accepting a claim, and was willing to have her own working assumption corrected by what the evidence actually showed (the recomposition-in-women claim, the exact whoosh mechanism, the joy-and-longevity framing narrowed to what was actually supportable).

**Resistance to anything paternalistic, even when well-intentioned.** Consistently caught and rejected persuasive or directive language, even in service of a good outcome (the MI-grounded language rules, "please reach out" flagged as an instruction, the guided-discovery onboarding design explicitly avoiding stated conclusions in favor of the user reaching them independently).

**Notices when something is technically correct but still feels wrong.** The clearest, most repeated pattern across the whole project — catching subtle experiential problems that pass functional testing (the vague-ack confusion, the mismatched grief/eating-disorder card, the silent-logging trust gap, the fourth-wall break). Usually surfaces as "that's not quite right" before being able to fully articulate why — the instinct arrives before the explanation.

**Willing to slow down and re-scope rather than ship something not fully stood-behind, even under real time pressure.** Held firm on proper safety investigation over quick patches multiple times, despite a genuinely tight timeline. (14 August 2026: with phase 4 done, chose to pull the MI guided-discovery activity flow out into its own dedicated later piece rather than let it be folded into the already-large onboarding phase as a follow-up — it "deserves the same dedicated attention as the safety language work, not to be folded into an already-large phase as an afterthought." The judgment is protecting the standard a piece of work is built to over the convenience of bundling it where it happens to be adjacent — and matching the depth of attention to the weight and sensitivity of the work, not to where it falls in the schedule.)

**Matches the strength of a guarantee to how much rides on the outcome.** When the correctness of something central is at stake, prefers an enforced mechanism over a well-intentioned instruction — willing to accept a little extra machinery to buy a real guarantee rather than trusting a softer approach to hold. (14 August 2026: on the MI guided-discovery gate, chose Option B — a route-enforced `readyToReflectLevel` field that structurally stops the deterministic level-echo from firing before the evocation has run — over the simpler Option A of just instructing the model to withhold the level, specifically *because* the discovery is the feature's whole value, so it warranted a route-level guarantee rather than trusting the prompt to behave. The reasoning was explicit: match enforcement strength to how much rides on the result. Note the contrast with the equipment step the same day, where button-driven capture plus AI-generated warmth was the right call — there, less rode on any single turn, so a lighter mechanism fit.)

**Prefers building real, working things over polished narrative before it's true.** Explicitly deferred marketing and content creation until the product itself is something she can honestly stand behind — "no point starting until I have a product I can stand behind."

**Designs for the degraded real-world state, not the ideal demo — and reframes the calmer choice as the stronger one, not a fallback.** Chooses the option that stays graceful when conditions are bad over the more ambitious one that only looks better when everything works. (18 August 2026: rejected a stroke-trace / line-drawing loading animation for the logo in favour of a gentle breathing pulse on the complete mark. The deciding reasoning was resilience under real conditions — a trace implies visible *progress toward completion*, so a stalled connection leaves a visibly broken, half-drawn mark on screen, whereas a pulse never implies completion and stays calm and whole even mid-stall. Explicitly named it as "genuinely the more on-brand choice under real conditions, not a fallback from the more ambitious idea." The pattern: judge a design by how it behaves in the failure case, and don't treat the quieter option as a compromise when it's actually the more robust one. Sits alongside "matches the strength of a guarantee to how much rides on the outcome" — both are about building for what actually happens, not the happy path.)

**Separates her own context from the product's requirements, and treats the leak as a defect to correct rather than a harmless example.** As the design-time example user, her own training context is the nearest source of concrete detail — which makes it the easiest thing to mistake for a product fact. She catches this and corrects it at the root. (19 August 2026: posterior-chain work had spread through the project's examples — the sample Almanac plan, the RDL safety note, a prominent group in the coverage workbook — and she named it plainly as her own training context, "not, and never was, a product focus for Unflump," then required that no requirement, coverage bar, acceptance test, or build assumption rest on it and that surviving references be explicitly labelled illustrative. The pattern is not modesty about her own preferences; it is recognising that a product built to serve "the full range of goals" is quietly narrowed every time the founder's own case is left standing as an unlabelled example. Note the verification instinct alongside it: the correction included checking whether code, schema, or migrations had absorbed the assumption, rather than assuming documentation was the only place it could have spread.)

**Scopes what an open question actually gates, rather than letting one unresolved item block everything adjacent to it.** When something is genuinely unresolved, she asks what specifically depends on it — and lets the rest proceed. (19 August 2026: the Romanian-deadlift variants were still unconfirmed when the $229 licensing decision came up. Rather than treating "an open item exists" as a reason to wait, she separated the two: the RDL preview governs the safety copy for *one movement*, while the purchase rests on broad movement-pattern coverage, which was confirmed. The open item stayed tracked as open rather than being absorbed into the purchase decision or quietly dropped to justify proceeding. The pattern is precision about dependency — neither over-blocking on an unrelated unknown, nor pretending the unknown is resolved because it is inconvenient. Sits opposite "willing to slow down and re-scope rather than ship something not fully stood-behind": both come from the same judgment, which is about locating what actually rides on a decision, not a general disposition toward caution or speed.)

**Attaches the abort condition to the authorisation, and stages the work so that using it stays cheap.** When approving a direction she is not certain about, she does not simply say yes and wait to see what happens. She names *in advance* what would make it wrong, delegates the judgment of whether that condition has been met, and requires it reported **before** anything is built. (31 August 2026: on adding a reputable-web-source layer to fill the gaps in the exercise classification data. The authorisation carried its own limit in the same breath — *"only use this if the sources can genuinely be trusted (established, reputable fitness/anatomy references, not arbitrary web content), and flag clearly if this introduces meaningful uncertainty or risk of pulling in unreliable information"* — alongside a separate instruction to *"report back honestly on feasibility and risk before implementing."* A three-exercise pilot found that two of the three returned muscle lists the reputable sources did not actually state: plausible content generated by inference and handed back **with citations attached**. She cancelled the layer outright and restated the reasoning in her own terms — it "produces confident-looking fabrication disguised as citation, which is worse than an honest AI classification," and it fails hardest exactly where the gap is, since the web documents well only the common exercises that would have been classified confidently anyway. Total cost of the wrong direction: nothing.)

Three things make this distinct from "evidence over assumption, including correcting her own instinct." That pattern is about *accepting* a correction once it arrives. This one is about **building the correction path in advance** — specifying the failure condition at the moment of approval, when it is free, rather than discovering it after the work exists, when it is not. The staging is what does the real work: separating "look into this" from "build this" is what made abandoning it cost nothing. And the reversal was total rather than partial — no attempt to salvage a reduced version of something that had just been shown to be unsound.

Worth noting what she did **not** do: she reaffirmed this direction twice before the pilot ran, over a recommendation against it. So this is not indecision or a weak commitment hedged in advance. The instruction was genuine and the abort condition was equally genuine, attached to it from the start — which is precisely what let strong commitment and fast reversal coexist without either being a performance.

**Will not decide on authority she cannot check — asks for the reasoning to be made legible first, then decides.** Presented with an expert recommendation, she separates the preference she already holds from the argument she has been given, and declines to act on the second until she can actually evaluate it. (31 August 2026: on the tagline typeface. Given a head-to-head and a set of trade-offs, she answered: *"I prefer sans as it all looks more coherent but I don't fully understand the implications you outlined."* Two things in one sentence — a clear preference, and a refusal to ratify it on borrowed reasoning. Once the three implications were restated plainly — that wide letter-spacing breaks words into loose letters at small sizes; that a light geometric sans can read as institutional, the one register the Brand Voice rejects; and that Comfortaa and Work Sans share a construction so the lockup reads as one type system — her own stated reason turned out to be the third of these, and the one a typographer would have led with.)

The tell is that this costs a round trip and she takes it anyway, rather than either deferring to the recommendation or overriding it on taste. It is adjacent to "resistance to anything paternalistic," but pointed inward: not *don't tell me what to do*, but *don't let me agree to something I can't independently assess*. Note also the sequence — the instinct ("more coherent") arrived before the vocabulary for it, which is the positive form of "notices when something is technically correct but still feels wrong": the same judgment running forwards instead of catching an error.

**Treats a generic answer as a failure even when it is not wrong — the test is whether the data already held the real one.** Noticing a leg-soreness question answered with "a heavier session in the last day or two" while 40 kg deadlifts and 90 minutes of ballet sat in the previous day's activity log, she did not read it as a wrong reply to be corrected. She read it as a *hollow* one, and turned it straight into a standing rule: a symptom query triggers a check of the prior 24-48 hours before anything is said.

The distinguishing move is the standard she applied. A reply that is defensible in isolation still fails if the app already stored what would have made it specific — "could not know" is forgivable, "did not look" is not, and from the person's side the two are indistinguishable. That is a higher bar than accuracy, and it is the bar an app whose proposition is *knowing you* actually has to clear.

She also recognised the shape rather than the instance: this is the Almanac insight rule (connect a result to a different data type across time) applied in real time, so it went into the spec as a general behaviour rather than a fix for soreness questions. Adjacent to "separates her own context from the product's requirements" — a lived annoyance became a product rule, not an anecdote.

**Names the class of evidence required before an irreversible step, not just that evidence is required.** Told that the Vercel rename gate was satisfied because the app was configured to call `api.selodia.app` and that domain resolved, she did not accept it: *"Need observed traffic, not just configuration evidence."* The rename went ahead only once the runtime logs showed five real POSTs arriving on that host and none on the old one. (3 September 2026.)

The distinction she drew is the whole of it. Configuration evidence says what *should* happen; observed evidence says what *did*. For a reversible change the first is plenty, and she has never asked for more. For a step that would break every installed build still calling the old host, she raised the standard specifically — and raised it before the step, not after it failed. The same move appears earlier the same day on the QuickLogBar: *"Investigate this first — report what's happening before fixing anything,"* where the suspected cause turned out to be wrong and the real one was worse.

This is adjacent to "will not decide on authority she cannot check," but it is pointed at proof rather than reasoning: not *explain it until I can evaluate it*, but *that is the wrong kind of proof for a thing this hard to undo*. It also runs in her own direction, not only outward — she carries unfinished work forward as Queued or Outstanding rather than letting a close-out record only what went well.

**Accepts a substandard state on purpose, and writes the expiry condition into the record at the same moment.** Told that a spoken turn measured ~4.4 seconds, she neither waved it through nor stopped to fix it. She dictated a spec note that did four things at once: stated the number, stated why it is tolerable *for this specific use* (“the use case is logging hands-free, not real-time conversation”), stated flatly that it is **“not permanently acceptable”**, and attached a figure to a named future event — *“under 3s before App Store submission.”* (4 September 2026.)

The move is the fourth part. A known-imperfect state that is merely accepted becomes permanent by default, because nothing in the record ever says when it stopped being acceptable — and the person best placed to notice is the one who accepted it, at the moment they accept it, when the reasoning is still fresh and free to write down. Binding it to App Store submission rather than a date is what gives it teeth: a date slips quietly, a gate does not.

Note what she did *not* do, which is the other half. She did not let the compromise expand to cover adjacent things: the same session’s optimisation work carried a standing instruction that **“the safety block stays untouched”**, repeated unprompted across three separate rounds of latency work. So the tolerance is scoped as tightly as the acceptance — this number, for this reason, until this event, and nothing else moves.

Adjacent to “attaches the abort condition to the authorisation,” and pointed differently. That pattern governs work whose *soundness* is uncertain, and names what would make it wrong. This one governs work already known to be below standard, and names what would make it no longer tolerable. Both are the same underlying instinct — write the limit down while writing it down is still cheap.

**Reads the actual requirement rather than the cautious version of it, and holds that reading when it is contradicted.** Told the ElevenLabs application video needed 60 to 90 seconds of the app running with voice, which made the whole submission wait on voice reliability that does not exist yet, she said plainly: *"the video is of me though, I can do it tomorrow at Wework"* and then, when that was not immediately accepted, *"the video of the app working must come later in the process."* She was right. The form asks for a short video introducing the founder, the product and the ElevenLabs use. Nothing in it requires a demonstration.

What makes this a pattern rather than a lucky call is that she had already conceded the point once, days earlier, when the same argument was made more confidently than it deserved. Coming back to it, restating it twice, and not being talked out of it the second time is the harder half. She also caught the next layer unprompted: *"i think less, it says max 3"* — a maximum is a ceiling, not a target, and eleven entries watched back to back reward brevity.

The general shape: **an overstated constraint quietly becomes a permanent blocker.** Three separate times in one day, a caution phrased as an absolute had to be walked back — the video dependency, "changing owner or slug would fork the project" when only the projectId matters, and a flat "turn analytics off" in place of the real argument, which was about timing. Each had been written down somewhere authoritative, and each was then being quoted back as a reason not to act. Her instinct in every case was to ask what the requirement actually says rather than accept the safe restatement of it.

The counterpart worth noting: she does not resolve these by overruling. She asks *why not*, repeatedly and without heat, until either the reason survives contact or it does not.

## How this gets built

At natural session-close points, add any new decision moment that reveals a genuine pattern — not every decision, only ones that add real signal about how Ruth thinks, distinct from the specific product or process choice itself.

**Sizes the response to a discovered mistake by what it actually changes, and locates the lesson in the timeline rather than in the act.** Told that 2,310 frames extracted from licensed clips had been sent to a model on 28 August, in plain breach of terms nobody read until 9 September, she did not escalate, remediate, or treat the finding as a crisis to be processed. She established the shape of it in one pass — no bad faith, the vendor had no muscle metadata so filling that gap was ordinary daily build work, and the licence was not read because there was no particular reason to think a formal one existed — and then decided: *"I say we leave everything as is and just keep going."* Nothing deleted, nothing disclosed, the manifest kept.

Three things make this a judgment rather than an avoidance. She separated **the method from the artefact**: the breach was in how the data was produced, so deleting 770 rows of real work would have un-analysed nothing and cost the project a month. She converted the finding into a **forward position** rather than a backward one — the animatics are "just a starting point," and the natural moment to raise this with the vendor is inside a commercial conversation about producing what Selodía actually needs, not as a standalone confession about a one-off classification pass. And she treated **the record as the remedy**: *"once we realised we documented it."* The thing that makes a good-faith breach defensible later is a contemporaneous account of what happened and why, which is exactly what was produced.

The tell is what she did not spend time on. No relitigating the decision to buy, no audit of what else might be non-compliant, no apology loop — and no pretending it had not happened either, which is the failure mode on the other side. Note the contrast with "willing to slow down and re-scope rather than ship something not fully stood-behind": that pattern spends time freely when the *quality of the work* is at stake. This one refuses to spend it when the outcome is already settled and the only thing left to buy is the appearance of having taken it seriously.

**Stops work that a pending decision might invalidate, rather than banking it as progress.** Offered a 750-row alias review that would visibly improve the demonstration coverage number, she declined it in the same breath as agreeing it was worth doing eventually: *"let's just keep building, we can review all this at 49, it's not holding us back"* — then, on being told the reasoning back, *"no point grafting for no reason, exactly."* Item 49 may replace most of the mobility library outright, and aliases curated against clips that get superseded are effort spent twice.

This looks like the RDL-scoping pattern above and is its mirror image. There, an unresolved item was prevented from blocking work that did not depend on it. Here, *available* work is refused because it depends on something unresolved — the same question asked in the other direction: not "what does this block?" but "what could make this pointless?" The distinguishing detail is that the deferred work was neither hard nor risky. It was simply downstream of a decision not yet made, and she declined to bank visible progress as a substitute for making that decision first.

**Holds anything in her name to the true size of the thing, and hears the difference between meaning and mechanism.** Across the ElevenLabs application on 11 September she corrected the copy again and again, and every correction pulled it towards what was actually true or actually felt, never towards what sounded stronger. Her story: *"I actually started strength training jan 2025"*, replacing a tidy "right as I turned 40" that was a year out. The product: "it isn't a diet app, a fitness tracker or a symptom checker" was *"not entirely true, it does those things"*; the point was *"holding the thread between all the parts."* The register: a closing answer was *"way too cocky and not my voice"*, and a birthday line was *"overkill."* A real app screenshot, flat weight after a hard session, came out because out of context it *"seems like a diet app"* and implied her goal was weight when it was body fat.

The second half recurred in the drafting. Twice she stopped copy that described *"what the code does"* (energy recalculation, smart-scale tracking, petals growing from the tip) and asked for *"the UX and the sentiment and philosophy"* instead. The Health Flower slide only worked once it said why six petals matter, not how they are drawn.

The shape: drafted copy drifts upward, towards bigger claims, a cleaner story and more mechanism, and she pulls it back to scale even when the inflated version reads better. An application whose whole strength is that everything in it is true treats a flattering inaccuracy as a cost, not a gain. This is the outward-facing twin of "reads the actual requirement rather than the cautious version of it": that pattern refuses overstated constraints, this one refuses overstated claims.

**Decides visual questions by comparison, and converges in small, named corrections.** Shown every recovered version of the Health Flower, she said plainly that none was the one she remembered, and did not settle for the nearest. Given variants side by side, she steered by deltas against what was on the page: *"ovals but thinner and more airy"*, then *"basically A but fatter in the middle ... but the colours of D"*, then *"between 1 and 2, slightly thinner tips and fuller middle"*, then *"too pointy, let's go with 1"*, then *"no, 2!"* Five rounds, each one a precise instruction relative to an option she could see. (11 September 2026.)

Two things make this a pattern and not indecision. She never tried to specify the design in the abstract; she needed options in front of her and answered each round with a comparison, which is faster and more exact than any description would have been. And her final reasons were brand values rather than taste, *"the nicest and lightest and most calm and minimalist"*, which is the brief for Selodía stated as a verdict. The practical lesson for anyone working with her on visuals: show a small labelled set, side by side, including the previous favourite for reference, and expect the answer as a delta.

**Orders the build by what each part is for: the destination before the channel that feeds it.** With voice needed for demo day on 21 October and next in the queue, she moved it behind the Almanac in one sentence: *"we move to almanac build before voice as that's what voice is for, to populate almanac properly."* The same day she held the weekly roundups, which the notification build had just made possible, because *"the current spec is too thin and the app has evolved significantly since early scoping"* and *"roundups land in the Almanac as weekly entries"*: the thing a roundup is written into had to be designed before the roundup. And when a good new idea surfaced mid-build (saved workouts appearing as quick taps, because *"gym could literally hold anything"*), it was recorded for Movement and the answer was *"stick with order."* (12 September 2026.)

What makes this distinct from "stops work that a pending decision might invalidate" is the question being asked. That pattern refuses work that sits downstream of a decision not yet made. Here both pieces of work were ready to start, and the choice was which comes first; she answered it by asking what each one is *for*, and built the part that gives the other its meaning. Voice without a well-shaped Almanac would be a fast way to fill a store that does not yet say anything; built the other way round, the voice work is aimed at a known target.

The tell is that it overrode a real deadline rather than yielding to it. Demo day made voice the obvious next thing, and the purpose order won anyway. Adjacent to "willing to slow down and re-scope rather than ship something not fully stood-behind", but pointed at sequence rather than standard: nothing about voice was below par, it was simply the wrong thing to perfect first.
